**Criteria for the later detailed content edit**

For a later detailed edit, record the original-file selection, actual review viewport, the portion viewed and any access/login interruption. Distinguish the project's own stated context from visual interpretation. A screenshot can substantiate a composition or a visible sequence; it cannot establish an entire delivery standard.

| Review dimension | Record concrete evidence | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Occasion and context | Stated project title, event type, place and captions; use “not stated” where absent | Guessing culture, nationality, budget, relationships or location from appearance |
| People and moments | Observed gestures, interactions, portrait direction and attention to people outside the central couple | Inferring personality, client satisfaction or a relationship that the source does not identify |
| Light and colour | Visible treatment across the frames actually inspected; daylight/interior/night examples if present | Claiming consistency across unseen images or treating an intentional look as a technical defect without evidence |
| Composition | Image scale, subject placement, use of environment, intimate versus wider frames and unusual viewpoints | Calling all work “cinematic,” “luxury” or “documentary” without specifying what was observed |
| Sequence | Whether the public edit provides a comprehensible progression or chiefly a set of portraits; repeated and missing context | Confusing a curated showcase with a chronological or complete delivered gallery |
| Potential site role | Candidate opening image type, story theme, supporting proof, portrait/example of guidance, place-led work | Selecting a final hero or claiming reuse permission before original files and approval |
| Content dependency | Missing title/context, current relevance, author credit, client permission, original files and approved story text | Filling gaps with invented couple stories or generic testimonials |

Use qualitative observations first. If a score is later helpful for comparing candidate assignments, define its criteria and apply them to comparable review coverage; do not manufacture a numeric quality ranking from unequal samples.

**Provisional implications from the expanded sample**

- **A wedding story can be more than a portrait showcase.** Malav & Pari provides observed links between couple portraits, ceremony/family context, evening light and group celebration. Request its fuller edit to evaluate those transitions and supporting coverage before selecting it as the main story.
- **Intimacy and movement are both visible in the couple work.** Mili & Saptrishi offers gentle meadow portraits; Vasya & Anthony moves between quiet closeness and playful coastal movement. These could support different rhythms within one photographer-led presentation. The photographs alone cannot establish the clients' experience or the method used to guide them.
- **Colour should come from the work before a theme.** The sampled work ranges from soft clothing/landscape tones to strong ceremony colour, with black-and-white images in some sequences. A monochrome, pastel or warm-neutral website must be tested against this actual range; no single sampled scene selects the brand palette.
- **Environment is useful context, not a blanket destination claim.** Goa is an explicitly named project. The meadow/hill and coastal scenes in other projects provide visual settings without establishing named places, travel coverage or commercial positioning.
- **Current portfolio currency remains unresolved.** Recorded publication dates are from 2023 and 2024. Representative current originals and the assignments the photographer wants more of still need confirmation. The accessible index is a useful research sample, not necessarily his preferred current edit.

These implications are hypotheses, not a chosen brand position. International references should inform how the real work is presented, not overwrite its colour, people, context or strengths.
