# Browser review: photographs, stories and enquiry journeys

Reviewed 15 October 2026 using the Codex in-app browser. This supplements the [Indian inventory](INDIAN-BENCHMARK-RESEARCH.md) and [international inventory](INTERNATIONAL-AND-PERFORMANCE-RESEARCH.md), which together cover 24 sites. Twelve of those sites received the browser inspection below. Public selections were viewed; no enquiry, booking, newsletter, login, purchase or other submission was made.

## Method and limits

Read rendered page/accessibility content and inspected screenshots. Followed selected story and contact destinations, including Nirav's story selector and Artistick's enquiry page. Initial desktop screenshots were approximately 1280×720; later ordinary side-panel screenshots were 523×804. Artistick's contact page also received a 390×844 emulated layout check, confirmed by DOM dimensions. This is a visual/journey sample, not certification on physical phones or a cross-browser test suite.

The in-app viewport override did not initially change the actual layout dimensions. A tab-scoped DevTools emulation subsequently did; the native screenshot surface still had cropping/scaling quirks. Consequently no pixel-level responsive defect is asserted from those captures. Temporary emulation/viewport overrides were cleared. Device validation for our future site is specified separately.

Several initial screenshots showed incomplete image/reveal states; later screenshots showed the photographs. This establishes an observed intermediate state, not a load-time measurement, persistent failure or evidence that the site is slow for ordinary users. No network waterfall, Lighthouse, CrUX, field INP or booking conversion was measured. Accessible-tree labels provide clues, not a complete accessibility conformance audit.

## Findings that change our plan

1. **The best inspiration is a mechanism, not a palette.** Specific captions, fuller assignments, clarity about the person doing the work, and useful coverage information can travel across visual styles. They still require our own evidence.
2. **The portfolio edit is a major design task.** Choose the images and sequence before polishing components. A small set of excellent, representative stories can be more convincing than an undifferentiated wall of photographs.
3. **The immediate view must already work.** Rich transitions can decorate an available experience; essential work, navigation and enquiry should not wait for an entrance animation or third-party player.
4. **A lightweight page can still be cognitively heavy.** Long forms, unfamiliar labels and too many categories create effort even when bytes are low. Keep the first enquiry proportional to what the responder actually needs.
5. **A strong style does not prove commercial impact.** Our hypotheses must later be checked against real enquiry quality, response, quote and booking outcomes.
6. **Polish includes truthful content and recovery.** Replacement notes belong in our review version. Fake testimonials, contradictory terms, unfinished production copy and simulated success never belong in the public release.

The owner's [own portfolio assessment](CONTENT-ASSESSMENT.md) remains the primary creative anchor. Benchmarks supply alternatives and cautions; they do not decide the photographer's business model or identity.
