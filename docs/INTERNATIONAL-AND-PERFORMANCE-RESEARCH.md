International photography references and a fast-experience architecture

Research checked 15 October 2026. This extends the [initial research brief](../PHOTOGRAPHER-RESEARCH-AND-STRATEGY-2026-10-14.md) and informs [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md). It is research and a set of proposals, not a selected design, stack or authorization to build.

**What was actually checked**

Nine accessible international photographer websites were read through public HTTP responses, including their available navigation and selected story, approach, biography and contact pages. Their current URLs and content were checked rather than relying on historical reputations. No forms were submitted, accounts accessed, media downloaded or website code generated. Rendered appearance, animation, keyboard behavior and mobile usability require the separate browser audit; HTML alone does not establish those results. JavaScript-rendered material may be absent from this inspection.

No Lighthouse, WebPageTest, CrUX or field-performance measurements were taken for these websites. This report does not rank them by speed, infer conversion rates or claim their design causes their commercial success. Testimonials and credentials below are observations of what a site publishes, not independent verification of those claims.

**What the comparison changes in our thinking**

The valuable common pattern is authored work plus evidence of a real working relationship. A generic luxury headline would erase the distinction between these practices. For Seven Steps, investigate which photographs and working behaviors are actually recognisable: family attention, ritual timing, deliberate portrait direction, use of colour, or an ability to make people comfortable. The previous public sample suggests possibilities, not a final answer.

There are two different kinds of proof. A tightly edited sequence demonstrates taste and an artistic point of view. A coherent assignment demonstrates coverage and consistency. Present both at the appropriate depth. A hundred-image homepage neither guarantees completeness nor helps someone quickly understand the work.

Practical information can be art-directed without being hidden. Service fit, where the photographer works, who attends, what preparation is like and how to enquire can be concise and attractive. Research the actual answers before writing them. Do not borrow another photographer's prices, scarcity, publication logos or turnaround promises.

Seven Steps' first likely job is to turn existing interest into informed, received enquiries. Keep public project URLs shareable in WhatsApp, readable on an ordinary phone and useful when opened directly. The quality of the first photograph and the clarity of the next step matter more than forcing every visitor through the same entrance animation.

**Candidate experience architecture (proposal for concept comparison)**

1. **An immediate photographic signature.** Deliver one representative photograph with a deliberate mobile crop, the brand and enough real language to identify the service. A considered composition, unusual pairing or photographic sequence can provide the memorable idea. Do not make seeing the first image depend on a preloader, JavaScript animation, web font, video player or a scroll gesture.
2. **A small edit that opens into real stories.** Introduce a few distinct assignments; each has a meaningful name/context and its own address. Give a visitor permission to look deeper. The editorial sequence should mix scale and pacing deliberately, not turn every photograph into an identical card.
3. **A story page with progressive depth.** Start with context and a strong frame, then a considered sequence of atmosphere, people, rituals and portraits that the actual assignment supports. Offer further work without fetching a whole archive on entry. A lightbox can be an enhancement; the page itself must remain a usable gallery.
4. **Evidence attached to the relevant claim.** Put an approved account of camera comfort near the explanation of portrait guidance; an approved complete wedding near a claim about coverage. Keep public testimonials attributable and truthful. A generic five-star strip cannot establish the same detail.
5. **A short, useful way to enquire.** Carry the chosen project's reference into the enquiry. Ask the smallest useful set of details for a real response; date/occasion/location, preferred contact and a short message are starting hypotheses. Optional interest in a particular story can replace a long compulsory essay. Confirm scope with the responder.
6. **A direct path for returning visitors.** Work, the person/process and contact remain discoverable without restarting the visual introduction. Preserve browser back behavior, deep links and image context when moving between stories.

This is compatible with several visual directions and implementation approaches. It does not select a framework, CMS, hosting provider, font, palette, carousel or final sitemap.

**Primary guidance that constrains the craft**

|---|---|---|
| [P1: web.dev, Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) | “Good” thresholds are LCP ≤2.5s, INP ≤200ms and CLS ≤0.1, assessed at the 75th percentile, separating mobile and desktop. | Treat fast loading, responsive actions and stable layout as outcomes to measure. An asset budget or a high lab score does not prove field success. |
| [P2: web.dev, Optimize LCP](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-lcp) | Make the LCP resource discoverable in initial HTML; do not lazy-load the LCP image. Request priority and discovery timing matter. | The main photograph should be a real image resource available early. Do not wait for a JS slideshow to inject it, and do not preload every possible hero. |
| [P3: web.dev, Responsive images](https://web.dev/learn/images/responsive-images) and [AVIF](https://web.dev/learn/images/avif) | `srcset` and `sizes` communicate candidate resources and rendered size; format choice affects quality and bytes. | Generate useful sizes and encode from originals. Compare AVIF/WebP against the actual photographs, with an appropriate fallback. Assess faces, fine clothing, gradients and low-light grain; do not blindly apply one compression setting. |
| [P4: web.dev, Fonts](https://web.dev/articles/font-best-practices) | Font delivery/rendering can delay text or cause shifts. WOFF2, subsetting and deliberate loading help; self-hosting is not automatically faster. `optional` and `swap` have different tradeoffs. | Use a small licensed font set, match fallback metrics and keep text visible. Preserve all glyphs actually needed, including names and any approved Devanagari content. Test brand-heading font arrival rather than hiding the page. |
| [P5: web.dev, Lazy loading video](https://web.dev/articles/lazy-loading-video) and [Chrome guidance, Third-party facades](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/performance/third-party-facades) | Defer media/player work until useful; a lightweight poster/button can load an embedded player on interaction. **Proposed performance budgets for the first concepts**

These are **project proposals**, not measured values for Seven Steps or the references, and not universal standards. They are deliberately ambitious enough to make tradeoffs visible. Refine them with representative permitted images and the actual maintenance/hosting constraints before adopting them. KiB/MiB are binary units; measure compressed network transfer, not uncompressed source-directory size. Count both first-party and third-party work.

| Budget | Candidate target | Tradeoff / interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Essential content | Brand, initial photograph, navigation, story context and contact information available without waiting for application JS | Rich interactions enhance this baseline. A normal server-handled form or clear direct-contact fallback can preserve a useful path if JS fails. This is an architecture proposal, not a framework choice. |
| Critical bytes before main image can render | Aim for ≤350 KiB on the representative mobile landing page | Prioritise discovery/order as well as bytes. The budget includes the needed document/styles, main photograph and truly critical font resources; late loading can still fail LCP at this weight. |
| Cold entry total | Aim for ≤900 KiB transferred during the first 10 seconds, no scrolling or interaction, at 390×844 CSS pixels | Includes lazy images the browser fetches nearby, fonts, JS, CSS, analytics and other automatic startup. This prevents hiding startup cost outside a narrowly defined “above fold” number. An entry story page should target the same first-load envelope. |
| First photograph | Mobile target approximately 120–220 KiB; desktop approximately 250–450 KiB, at justified display dimensions | These are encoding targets, not fixed caps regardless of content. Choose a mobile crop deliberately; preserve faces, gestures and image integrity. If quality suffers, revisit crop/resolution/format or nearby requests before raising total weight. |
| Full-page image use | Homepage deliberate full scroll target ≤3 MiB; a curated 25–35-image story target ≤6 MiB, excluding expressly played video or optional full-resolution viewing | A visitor who asks for a story may spend more data. Spread requests with reading progression. Do not load full original files or all other stories in the background. The number of images remains an editorial decision. |
| JavaScript | Initial transfer target ≤80 KiB compressed; core content remains available when it fails | Budget covers framework/runtime, gallery, analytics and UI code combined. Defer optional lightbox/video code to intent. Framework capability is not a reason to consume the budget. |
| Styles/document | Initial CSS target ≤30 KiB compressed; HTML target ≤35 KiB compressed | These are diagnostic limits, not reasons to remove semantic structure or inline enormous image placeholders. Avoid shipping unrelated templates and full component libraries. |
| Fonts | Prefer 1–2 families and ≤100 KiB initially across **all actually requested font files, weights, styles and language subsets combined** | A variable font is not automatically smaller for a few weights. Latin, Devanagari, punctuation and name coverage can trigger additional files; all count. Preserve required glyphs, combining marks and shaping behavior rather than cutting language support to fit a number. A distinctive type choice must coexist with an attractive, stable fallback. |
| Automatic video | 0 video/player bytes before explicit intent in the default mobile experience; poster counted in image budget | This chooses immediate photographic impact over a forced background film. If a later concept makes a strong case for ambient video, compare it against the still version on identical devices/networks and retain user/static controls. |
| Third parties | No initial social-feed, map, chat or film iframe; any chosen analytics is included in the JS/entry budget | Use current linked editorial content rather than a live feed by default. Add services when they solve a concrete job and their measured cost is understood. |
| Interaction and stability | Good field INP ≤200ms and CLS ≤0.1; no layout movement caused by missing image dimensions or late fonts | A navigation/lightbox test is needed; a page-load score cannot establish interaction responsiveness. Reserve image/media space, preserve focus and browser history. |
| Motion | Prefer user-triggered state changes; a small set of short transitions; all essential content visible in reduced-motion mode | Do not use scroll-jacking, scroll-gated text or an entrance overlay as the only way to reach content. A memorable composition should still work without movement. |

The critical-path and cold-entry budgets are complementary. A 900 KiB entry budget does not mean 900 KiB should block the first photograph. Conversely, a light hero does not excuse several megabytes of automatic work a moment later. Keep a visible budget exception with the reason, image-quality comparison, measured impact and an alternative; do not silently abandon the target.

The per-resource targets are diagnostic ceilings, not an allocation that can all be consumed at once. Their upper values can exceed the critical total: a 220 KiB image + 100 KiB fonts + 30 KiB CSS + 35 KiB HTML already equals 385 KiB. One coherent **illustrative**, unmeasured mobile allocation is HTML 25 KiB + CSS 22 KiB + all first-view font files/subsets 60 KiB + main image 190 KiB + other critical transfers 20 KiB = **317 KiB**, leaving 33 KiB below the proposed 350 KiB target. These must be the resources actually needed for the first view; any newly required font or script consumes the same allowance. In this example, another 60 KiB of deferred JS, 240 KiB of nearby photographs and 20 KiB of later font files would bring the cold-entry total to 637 KiB and total initially fetched fonts to 80 KiB. Count those later requests even if they do not block the first image.

At 1.6 Mbps, transferring 350 KiB alone takes approximately 1.8 seconds before accounting for response delays, request dependencies, decode and rendering. Thus the size target cannot guarantee a 2.5-second LCP. The browser may also fetch offscreen lazy images within the first ten seconds; actual requests determine the entry result. A 6 MiB story with 25–35 images allows roughly 175–246 KiB per image even before other page resources, so some detailed or low-light stories may need a shorter edit or a justified larger allowance. Do not damage skin, textile detail or the intended photographic treatment to hit a synthetic cap.

The 80 KiB initial-JS proposal may conflict with a managed site builder or CMS runtime. Evaluate maintenance cost and the measured experience together before choosing a provider or revising the target; no platform is selected or ruled out solely by this exploratory number. Large-screen/high-density image quality, responsive crop behavior and real interaction latency remain separate test requirements.

**How to test the proposals later without misleading ourselves**

- Use a representative current mid-range Android phone, iPhone Safari and desktop browsers; record actual device/browser versions. Include Chrome/Firefox desktop for ordinary navigation and keyboard checks. A powerful development laptop is not the mobile baseline.
- Make the controlled mobile profile explicit: propose 390×844 CSS pixels, approximately 1.6 Mbps down / 750 Kbps up / 150ms RTT and a documented CPU slowdown for a lab scenario. Network tools simulate differently; record the actual tool and configuration. This is a stress-test proposal, not a statement of the audience's typical network.
- Run at least three comparable cold-load runs and report their median and range, plus a warm revisit. Record location, cache state, tested version and timestamp. Inspect the waterfall, main-image discovery and image-quality result; do not rely only on the composite Lighthouse score.
- Test home, direct-to-story entry, work index and enquiry route separately. Script only meaningful interactions once implementation is authorized: mobile menu, open/close gallery, next image, return navigation and form validation/failure. Keyboard focus should return to the triggering control after a lightbox closes.
- Inspect 320 CSS-pixel reflow, 200%/400% zoom, long names, landscape phone, reduced motion, blocked third parties, slow images and JavaScript failure. Confirm that the main work and contact path remain understandable.
- Compare image variants at their actual display size and device pixel ratio using representative skin tones, fabric/jewellery detail, gradients, motion blur and low-light photographs. Avoid selecting the smallest file that destroys the photographer's treatment.
- After a permitted launch, use real-user metrics where appropriate and available. CrUX may have insufficient data for a small site; report “insufficient field data,” not a pass or failure. Assess mobile and desktop separately at the 75th percentile for all three Core Web Vitals.
- Measure business outcomes independently: received and suitable enquiries, actual response times, quote/booking outcomes and operational effort. No speed result or website-reference pattern establishes a revenue forecast.

**Recommended next use of this research**

Compare two or three Seven Steps concepts using the same representative assignment, the same buyer task and the same proposed performance envelope. Spend creative effort on the photographic edit, image relationships, recognisable voice, typography and the movement between overview and story. Challenge every effect that hides content, shifts attention away from the photographs or introduces automatic weight.

The international references demonstrate several viable emphases. They do not resolve Seven Steps' owner-only questions or dictate its visual language. The strongest direction should be recognisably about this photographer and effortless to understand on a normal phone, with visual and technical claims backed by the later rendered tests.
